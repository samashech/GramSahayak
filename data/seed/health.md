# GramSahayak Health Guide for ASHA and Anganwadi Workers (health)

Field guide for maternal and primary health. GramSahayak does not diagnose illness and does not give medicine doses. In any emergency, go to the nearest government health centre immediately.

## Emergency numbers
- **108** — Ambulance for any medical emergency (free).
- **102** — Pregnancy and newborn ambulance (Janani Express), free transport to and from the hospital for pregnant women and sick infants.
- **104** — Health helpline for advice and information.

## Antenatal care (ANC): 4 check-ups

### Schedule
Every pregnant woman should have at least 4 antenatal check-ups:
1. First visit: as soon as pregnancy is known, ideally within the first 12 weeks — register the pregnancy and get the Mother and Child Protection (MCP) card.
2. Second visit: between 14 and 26 weeks.
3. Third visit: between 28 and 34 weeks.
4. Fourth visit: between 36 weeks and delivery.
Under the Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Abhiyan (PMSMA), a free check-up by a doctor is available at government facilities on the 9th of every month for women in the second and third trimester.

### What happens at each check-up
Weight, blood pressure, haemoglobin (anaemia) test, urine test for sugar and protein, abdominal examination, Td (tetanus-diphtheria) vaccination, and iron-folic acid and calcium tablets as given by the ANM. Plan the place of delivery in a hospital and keep the 102 number ready.

## Pregnancy danger signs (go to hospital now)
If a pregnant woman has any of these signs, take her to the nearest health centre immediately and call 108 or 102:
- Bleeding from the vagina at any time in pregnancy.
- Fits (convulsions) or loss of consciousness.
- Severe headache with blurred vision.
- Swelling of the face and hands.
- High fever.
- Severe pain in the abdomen.
- Fast or difficult breathing.
- Baby moving less or not moving.
- Water breaking without labour pains.
- Labour pains lasting more than 12 hours.
Do not wait or try home remedies. Keep the MCP card with you on the way.

## Janani Suraksha Yojana (JSY)
JSY gives cash assistance for delivery in a government or accredited hospital. In rural areas of Low Performing States the mother gets Rs 1,400 and the ASHA gets Rs 600; in High Performing States (such as Maharashtra and Tamil Nadu) mothers from BPL, SC or ST households get Rs 700. The ASHA registers the pregnancy early, helps with the 4 ANC check-ups, arranges transport (102) and accompanies the woman to the hospital. Payment goes to the mother's Aadhaar-linked bank account. Janani Shishu Suraksha Karyakram (JSSK) additionally makes delivery, medicines, tests, food and transport free in government hospitals. Helpline: 104.

## Universal Immunisation Programme (UIP)
All vaccines are free at government health centres and Village Health and Nutrition Days. Follow the dates on the MCP card:
- At birth: BCG, OPV-0, Hepatitis B birth dose.
- 6 weeks: OPV-1, Pentavalent-1, Rotavirus-1, fIPV-1, PCV-1.
- 10 weeks: OPV-2, Pentavalent-2, Rotavirus-2.
- 14 weeks: OPV-3, Pentavalent-3, Rotavirus-3, fIPV-2, PCV-2.
- 9 to 12 months: Measles-Rubella (MR-1), JE-1 (in endemic districts), PCV booster, Vitamin A (first dose).
- 16 to 24 months: MR-2, JE-2, DPT booster-1, OPV booster.
- 5 to 6 years: DPT booster-2.
- 10 years and 16 years: Td.
- Pregnant women: Td-1 and Td-2 (or one booster if vaccinated in the last 3 years).
Mild fever or pain at the injection site is common. If a child has high fever, fits or difficulty breathing after vaccination, go to the health centre at once.

## Diarrhoea in children: ORS and Zinc
- Give ORS solution after every loose stool; prepare one packet in one litre of clean drinking water and use it within 24 hours.
- Give zinc tablets for 14 days, as provided and explained by the ASHA or ANM, even if diarrhoea stops earlier.
- Continue breastfeeding and normal food.
- Danger signs — take the child to the health centre now: blood in stool, child not able to drink or breastfeed, very sleepy or unconscious, sunken eyes, repeated vomiting, fits, or diarrhoea lasting more than 14 days.
- Prevention: handwashing with soap, safe drinking water, toilet use, exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months, rotavirus vaccine.

## Snake bite first aid
Snake bite is an emergency. Call 108 and take the person to the nearest hospital that has anti-snake venom (ASV) immediately.
- Keep the person calm and lying still; movement spreads venom faster.
- Keep the bitten arm or leg still and below the level of the heart, like a fracture, using a splint if available.
- Remove rings, bangles, watches, anklets and tight clothes near the bite.
- Note the time of the bite.
Do NOT:
- Tie a tight tourniquet or rope.
- Cut or suck the wound.
- Apply ice, herbs, chemicals or any home remedy.
- Go to a faith healer or waste time trying to catch or kill the snake.
Anti-snake venom is given free in government hospitals.

## Where to get help
- 108 ambulance, 102 pregnancy ambulance, 104 health helpline.
- ANM at the sub-centre / Ayushman Arogya Mandir, Medical Officer at the PHC.
