# GramSahayak Agriculture Guide (agri)

Advisory content for small and marginal farmers. GramSahayak never recommends a pesticide brand or a pesticide dose. For chemical control, always ask the Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK), the block agriculture officer or the Kisan Call Centre 1800-180-1551 (toll-free, 6 am to 10 pm, in local languages).

## Crop seasons

### Kharif (monsoon season)
Sown with the onset of the monsoon, June to July; harvested September to October. Main crops: paddy (rice), maize, cotton, soybean, groundnut, bajra, jowar, tur (arhar), moong and urad. Prepare fields before the rains, use certified seed, and enrol in crop insurance (PMFBY) before the Kharif cut-off date.

### Rabi (winter season)
Sown October to December after the monsoon; harvested March to April. Main crops: wheat, mustard, gram (chana), barley, lentil (masoor) and peas. Rabi crops need cool weather for growth and warm weather for ripening; timely sowing of wheat (November) reduces heat stress at grain filling.

### Zaid (summer season)
Short season between Rabi and Kharif, March to June, mainly with irrigation. Crops: watermelon, muskmelon, cucumber, vegetables, moong and fodder crops.

## Integrated Pest Management (IPM) basics
IPM means using several safe methods together and using chemicals only as a last step on expert advice:
1. Grow resistant or tolerant varieties recommended for your area.
2. Sow on time, keep correct spacing and use balanced fertiliser as per the Soil Health Card.
3. Inspect the field every week; use pheromone traps and light traps to monitor pests.
4. Protect natural enemies (ladybird beetles, spiders, birds); put up bird perches.
5. Remove and destroy badly affected plants and crop residues.
6. Use neem-based or biological products where advised.
7. If damage crosses the economic threshold, contact the KVK or Kisan Call Centre 1800-180-1551 for the correct product and dose. Never mix chemicals on your own; wear gloves and a mask while spraying.

## Yellow Rust of wheat (Peela Ratua)

### Symptoms
Bright yellow to orange powder in stripes along the leaf veins, first in patches in the field. The powder sticks to fingers or a white cloth when rubbed. It appears mostly from mid-December to February in cool (10-15 °C), humid weather in north India (Punjab, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, western Uttar Pradesh, Jammu). Severe attack reduces grain size and yield.

### What to do
- Grow rust-resistant wheat varieties recommended by the state agriculture university.
- Avoid excess nitrogen fertiliser and very early sowing in rust-prone areas.
- Survey the field regularly from December, especially near poplar or eucalyptus trees and in shaded areas.
- On seeing the first patches, inform the KVK or agriculture department immediately; a fungicide spray may be advised by them. Ask the KVK or Kisan Call Centre 1800-180-1551 for the exact product and dose.
- Do not confuse it with nutrient yellowing: rust leaves a powder on the finger, nutrient deficiency does not.

## Pink Bollworm of cotton (Gulabi Bondali)

### Symptoms
Rosette (half-open, twisted) flowers; small holes in green bolls; pinkish larvae inside bolls; damaged, stained lint and seeds; bolls open badly. The pest is most damaging from August to November, and survives in leftover bolls, stalks and stored seed cotton.

### What to do
- Sow on time with short-duration varieties; avoid late sowing and extending the crop into December-January.
- Install pheromone traps for pink bollworm from about 45 days after sowing to monitor moth catches.
- Pick and destroy rosette flowers and damaged bolls.
- After the last picking, remove and destroy crop residues (do not stack cotton stalks near the field) and let animals graze the field.
- Do not store seed cotton in the house for long; clean ginning mills and godowns.
- If trap catches or damaged bolls cross the threshold, contact the KVK or Kisan Call Centre 1800-180-1551 for advice on control. GramSahayak does not give pesticide names or doses.

## Fall Armyworm of maize

### Symptoms
Ragged holes and "window panes" on young leaves, sawdust-like droppings (frass) in the whorl, and larvae with an inverted "Y" mark on the head and four dots in a square near the tail. Most damaging in the first 30-45 days of the crop.

### What to do
- Deep ploughing before sowing and timely, uniform sowing in the area.
- Intercrop maize with pulses such as tur, moong or urad.
- Inspect the whorls twice a week; hand-pick and destroy egg masses and young larvae.
- Install pheromone traps and bird perches.
- Use neem-based or biological products if advised. For heavy infestation, ask the KVK or Kisan Call Centre 1800-180-1551.

## Paddy Blast

### Symptoms
Spindle-shaped (eye-shaped) spots on leaves with grey centres and brown edges; in neck blast the neck of the panicle turns black and breaks, giving empty or chaffy grains. Favoured by high humidity, cool nights, and excess nitrogen.

### What to do
- Grow blast-resistant varieties; use healthy seed and treat seed as advised by the KVK.
- Apply nitrogen in split doses, not all at once; avoid excess urea.
- Keep fields and bunds free of weeds; avoid water stress.
- On first symptoms, contact the KVK or Kisan Call Centre 1800-180-1551 for fungicide advice.

## Using the Soil Health Card
The Soil Health Card shows the nutrient status of your field (nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, organic carbon, pH, sulphur, zinc, iron, copper, manganese, boron, electrical conductivity) and gives crop-wise fertiliser and manure recommendations.
- Read the recommendation for the crop you are going to sow and buy fertiliser accordingly.
- Do not add extra urea "for greener leaves"; excess nitrogen increases pests and diseases.
- Add farmyard manure, compost or green manure to improve organic carbon.
- Get the soil tested again every 2 years. Card and help: soilhealth.dac.gov.in.
